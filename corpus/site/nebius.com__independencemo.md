## Project overview

Nebius is committed to transparency throughout the process and to building a project that delivers lasting benefits to the community.

## No increase to residential power rates

Nebius will pay the full cost for power and necessary infrastructure upgrades. Once built and operational, Nebius will become an industrial consumer in IPL’s region, which is currently largely residential. This would help to spread fixed costs across more energy use and ease pressure on rates for existing customers while simultaneously boosting the local power grid. A new, privately financed facility is being developed by Independence Power Partners LP (IPP) at the City’s retired Blue Valley Power Plant site to supply the grid with the power needed.

## Annual water use of an average restaurant

Due to the innovative closed-loop water cooling design, the facility will require minimal annual water needs.

## Built to fit in

We’ve designed this facility to be a good neighbor. That means keeping noise low, using sound-reduction technology, planting native landscaping on our property, and planning construction work to limit disruptions to daily life.

## Creates jobs and strengthens the local economy

The project expects to create more than a thousand construction jobs, support local contractors, and deliver high-tech employment.

## Delivers long-term community investment

The project would generate millions of dollars each year to support Independence and Ft. Osage schools, public safety, and community services, while expanding education and workforce opportunities.

## FAQs

Who is Nebius and why did you choose Independence for your flagship US location?

Nebius is a technology company building a next-generation public AI cloud computing platform. By building and operating AI factories across the U.S. and globally, we make AI happen, whether it’s AI for medical breakthroughs, AI for education, or AI for the workplace.

Headquartered in the Netherlands and listed on NASDAQ, Nebius has roughly 1,300 employees globally — across Europe, Israel, and multiple locations in the United States.

We see clear opportunities where Independence can play a leading role in the AI age and are excited about our investment in the site and community. This city offers a promising combination of infrastructure, workforce, and leadership that’s welcoming of large-scale economic development projects. Our goal is to build with the community by being transparent, listening to community priorities, and becoming the kind of long-term, trusted partner Independence deserves.

How large is this investment Nebius is making?

Nebius purchased roughly 400 acres at Rte. 78 and Little Blue Parkway for a data center campus with a total capacity of up to 1.2 GW. The site represents a multi-billion-dollar investment across four buildings and on-site electrical infrastructure. The four buildings on the Nebius site will be constructed over five to seven years and support more than 1,000 skilled construction jobs, along with approximately 130 permanent high-tech positions as the facilities come online.

How will the project be powered? Will it impact local electric rates for consumers?

Power rates are protected and will not be impacted by our project. Nebius pays its full cost of power to ensure families don’t bear our power costs and we also signed President Trump’s Ratepayer Protection Pledge, which affirms our commitment.

Specifically, this project is a 1.2 GW sized facility, with 800 megawatts (MW) of power from Independence Power and Light and 400 MW from on-site generation. Thanks to our project and contract with IPL, the 800 MW will come from a new, privately financed facility developed by Independence Power Partners (IPP) at the retired Blue Valley Power Plant site. The energy generated by IPL will be fed to the Southwest Power Pool grid and Nebius will then buy the energy from the grid at market rates. This means there is no cost to IPL rate payers and we are bringing new generation to the grid to support our power needs.

In fact, city and IPL leaders note that adding a large industrial customer to IPL’s largely residential customer base will help spread fixed system costs across more energy use, easing pressure on rates for existing customers.

Nebius is evaluating an on-site power generation solution for the future project phase and will update as plans are developed.

Why do you need generators?

Onsite generators are for emergencies only, such as a power outage, to support life safety function, safely monitor, and run the cooling equipment to enable a managed facility shut down. They will not be used to fully operate the facility in the event of an outage.

Each building is expected to have two diesel-power emergency generators, which is common for emergency back-up generation in commercial buildings. Generators will have sound enclosures for noise mitigation and on-site fuel tanks, with 6,200 gallon capacity, are designed with secondary containment. An acoustical study of these generators in conjunction with sound enclosures determined noise levels to be negligible.

Should a power failure occur, the buildings will use back-up generators for roughly 15 minutes to safely power down the hardware inside and allow employees to safety leave the building. They will also run for roughly one hour per month as part of routine maintenance and testing.

How much water will this project use?

Nebius uses closed-loop cooling systems across our AI factory locations. Closed loop water systems recycle the liquid continuously in pipes that are welded shut, meaning there is minimal chance for leaks or evaporation, and therefore additional water usage. The system requires an initial fill of approximately 1 million gallons of water per 200 MW.

If Nebius purchases the initial water fill from the City of Independence, Nebius and the City will coordinate on fill timing. The City of Independence produces roughly 28 million gallons of water a day, and Independence says it has ample capacity to provide this one-time need.

After the initial fill, the site will use between 450,000 to 600,000 gallons of water a year for staff bathroom use and landscaping. This is roughly the same amount of water as an office building, and less than a gas station.

What chemicals are you putting in the water? Where will they go?

Liquid inside closed loop cooling systems are a mix of mostly water and propylene glycol, which increases the ability to capture and remove heat. Propylene glycol is a safe, widely used product often found in commercial HVAC systems, airplane de-icing, and, even is FDA-approved for use in foods and cosmetics.

As it will be in a closed loop system that is welded shut, it is highly unlikely that a leak occurs and escapes containment. If it does, Nebius would follow all city, state, and federal regulations, including Clean Water Act, Environmental Protection Agency, and Missouri Department of Natural Resources protocols.

Has Nebius conducted a sound study and what are the findings?

A sound study was conducted in Spring 2026 and is a model to predict sound levels at and around the site, regardless of the time of year. The acoustician modeled the specific equipment that will be used on site, which includes 12-foot sound-absorptive walls around chillers, 15-foot sound-absorptive walls around dry coolers, low-noise fans specified for chillers, and acoustical enclosures for generators. Additionally, the models factored in the placement of buildings, temperatures the equipment would operate in, and topography of the site.

This approach enables us to ensure we are designing the complete four building site — including equipment, placement of acoustical sound walls, etc. — to be at a minimum compliant with the city’s noise ordinance levels.

Additionally, the sound models also considered dBC readings, which is not required by the City of Independence. dBA and dBC are both ways of expressing sound levels in decibels, but they differ in how they weight frequencies. dBA uses the A-weighting curve, which mimics how the human ear hears at normal sound levels. In contrast, dBC uses the C-weighting curve, which is much flatter and includes more low-frequency energy.

According to the study results, which shared in the July 2026 Community Advisory Board meeting, the Nebius site is predicted to be below the city’s required ordinance levels, which are 60 dBA during the day and 50 dBA at night.

In this sound study, the worst case conditions were modeled at a point closest to residential homes. The worst case models were for the hottest daytime and nighttime temperatures reached in the last 20 years — 107.6 degrees and 100.5 degrees, respectively. The study indicates the monitor closest to a residential area to be at 45 dbA during the day and 41 dBA at night; comparable to a suburban area at night.

What is the site lighting plan?

Our lighting plans are specifically designed to keep light facing downward and we use shielding to keep light focused on our facility, parking lot, and equipment yards for safety and security.

Will the site equipment cause vibrations that could be felt by nearby residences?

Our engineers design equipment to minimize any vibration and not be felt off-site.

What does construction look like? Will you use local or union labor?

We are proud to partner with ARCO Construction as our general contractor for the project’s

Phase 1. Our workforce is comprised of more than 99% of union labor from the local Building Trades. Construction is in phases and will last for five to seven years across the nearly 400 acres.

Nebius and ARCO are taking proactive measures to minimize disruption to the community during this time, such as deploying dust control solutions including three water trucks to keep the soil moist, adding additional signage to re-reroute construction traffic, shift scheduling to manage traffic, especially around school drop-off and pick-up time, and evaluating road improvements post-construction.

What happened to Bly Road?

When Nebius purchased the land from Northpoint in December 2025, it included plans for Northpoint to reroute the southern portion of Bly Road west, to exit onto Little Blue Parkway before it reaches the Nebius property line. Northpoint began the Bly Road rerouting in Spring 2026, and is expected to be done in the Fall of 2026.

Why did you request a tax abatement, and what approvals did you receive?

In March 2026, Nebius received approval from the Independence City Council on the Chapter 100 industrial development incentive plan application. Chapter 100’s are very typical for data center and advanced manufacturing projects of this scale in Missouri. We will continue to work closely with City Staff, the City Council, the Independence and Ft. Osage School Districts, Metropolitan Community College, and Mid-Continent Library to ensure our project delivers significant benefits for the community.

The City of Independence estimates this project will bring as much as $55 million dollars to its general fund every year, and a similar amount will go to Independence Power and Light every year. Additionally, the project is expected to generate more than $500 million dollars for both the Independence and Fort Osage School Districts over 20 years.

What commitments is Nebius prepared to make to the community?

Nebius is committed to being a responsible, long-term partner in the communities where we operate. We are in regular communication with city, school district, and community leaders about our project and opportunities for investment, including actioning our education-focused community benefits plan.

We’ve also established a Community Engagement Panel comprised of local residents, businesses and leaders. The panel will meet regularly and serve as an important avenue of communication for Nebius to share project updates and information, and in return, receive feedback from its community partners and representatives. Additionally, the City of Independence created a Nebius Community Advisory Board, with members appointed by the current city council, with quarterly meetings to hear updates on what Nebius is doing at both the site and in the community.

What are Nebius’ top priorities?

Nebius focuses on education and workforce development in Independence. We believe investing in training is essential to preparing residents for emerging careers and fostering innovation. As a global leader in artificial intelligence, Nebius brings access to advanced computing infrastructure and industry thought leaders, helping Independence unlock new opportunities for growth.

In May 2026, Nebius eliminated more than $270,000 in school lunch debt for students in both the Independence and Fort Osage School Districts. During our May 2026 groundbreaking, a Memorandum of Understanding with Metropolitan Community College was announced to advance workforce training programs.

Nebius is also dedicated to transparent community engagement and maintaining open communication, post-project approval. To demonstrate our ongoing commitment to partnership and responsible development, Nebius has both a Community Engagement Panel and a city-appointed Community Advisory Board. Additionally, Nebius participates in multiple community initiatives, including the Chamber of Commerce and the Truman Heartland Community Foundation.
