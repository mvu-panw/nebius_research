## The interview process

## Site Reliability Engineer

Typically 5 interview stages (up to 6 for senior or specialist roles).

You’ll start with a technical screening on Linux, networking, Kubernetes, and core DevOps concepts, plus a practical coding exercise.

Next is a hands-on troubleshooting interview focused on Kubernetes and infrastructure debugging, followed by a deeper technical assessment.

Senior candidates, or those with highly specialized expertise, may complete an additional systems architecture or specialist interview.

The final stage is a Technical Deep Dive into your experience, followed by introductions to the teams you may join and discussions with the hiring managers.

## Backend Engineer

Typically 5 interview stages (up to 6 for senior roles).

You’ll start with a coding interview, then two algorithm and programming rounds of increasing difficulty that test problem-solving, code quality, and technical depth.

Senior candidates take an additional systems design interview.

The process wraps with a Technical Deep Dive into one of your past projects, where we’ll discuss architectural decisions, trade-offs, and your technical impact, followed by a conversation with the hiring manager about the role and team.

## System Engineer

Typically 5 interview stages.

You’ll begin with a coding interview, solving practical algorithm and low-level programming problems, followed by two progressively more advanced coding rounds, exploring systems programming concepts in greater depth.

Next is a Technical Deep Dive tailored to your background, covering topics such as Linux internals, virtual memory, networking, containers, distributed systems, and other relevant areas.

The process closes with a discussion with the hiring manager about the team, projects and your experience.

## ML Engineer

Typically 4 interview stages (up to 5 for specialist roles).

You’ll start with a hands-on coding session in a Jupyter notebook, completing and debugging a small ML task to show your Python and ML fundamentals.

Depending on the role, you may then take part in a specialist interview on topics like LLMs, transformer architectures, or large-scale training.

This is followed by an algorithms interview and a Technical Deep Dive into one of your previous ML projects, focusing on your technical decisions, implementation and real-world impact.

## Frontend Engineer

Typically 4 interview stages.

Two JavaScript and algorithms coding rounds.

Senior candidates are also assigned a system design session –– architectural discussion using a digital whiteboard.

## Engineering Bootcamp

Find the right team, not just the right job.

Choosing the right team is just as important as choosing the right company. That’s why many engineers on our core cloud team start with Engineering Bootcamp, a 3-month rotational program designed to help you discover where you’ll have the biggest impact.

## How it works

- 

You’ve got the job — welcome to Nebius!

- 

Kick off with a 3-month Engineering Bootcamp.

- 

Rotate across up to four engineering teams, spending around 3-4 weeks with each team.

- 

Experience different projects, ways of working and team cultures.

- 

At the end of the Bootcamp, you and the teams make a joint decision on the best long-term fit.

## Why engineers love it

- Find your best fit: experience multiple teams before choosing the team where your skills, interests and ambitions align.

- Work on real projects: contribute to production systems from the very beginning.

- Accelerate your org knowledge: build knowledge across our engineering organization and work with engineers across different products and infrastructure early on.

## Boosting your chances

Think out loud. We care more about your thinking than a perfect answer. Walk us through your assumptions, the trade-offs you’re weighing, and why you’d choose one approach over another. Be ready to adapt if the problem changes and offer alternative solutions whenever possible, even if we’ve moved on.

Engineering fundamentals: strong algorithms, data structures, and systems thinking for your specialization. Practice on LeetCode or HackerRank, and brush up on Numbers Every Programmer Should Know, measuring algorithm complexity, tree traversals, working with stacks/queues/cycles, and asynchrony. SREs should also sharpen Linux and scripting skills. Check code for errors and readability before submitting.

Practical experience. Be ready to go deep on things you’ve actually built, debugged, and shipped, including what didn’t work and why. The decisions matter as much as the outcomes.

Collaborate with your interviewer. Don’t hesitate to ask for a hint. Interviewers are there to guide you through problems, not just score the result.

## FAQ

What should I expect in the Technical Deep Dive?

One of the final stages of our interview process is a 90-minute Technical Deep Dive about one significant project you’ve worked on. Rather than asking hypothetical questions, we want to understand what you actually built, the decisions you made, and the impact you had.

Pick your project

Choose a project where you were a key contributor and can clearly explain both the technical and business context in depth. Ideally it’s day-to-day work rather than a side project, and one that shows real scale and complexity.

It could be a product or feature you launched, a large infrastructure or platform change, a migration or modernization project, a performance or scalability improvement, an ML system or any project you’re particularly proud of.

What we’ll cover

We’ll go deep on this one project rather than wide across several, covering things like the problem you were solving, your role, the key trade-offs and decisions, the biggest technical challenges, how the solution was deployed and operated, and the results.

We’re most interested in your individual contribution, rather than the team’s overall achievement.

Be prepared to explain why you made particular decisions, what alternatives you considered, and what you learned along the way. It’s perfectly fine to reflect on decisions you would make differently today as we value thoughtful engineering and continuous learning.

What we’re listening for

Engineering judgement: how you approach complex problems and make technical decisions

Technical depth: the most technically challenging aspects of your project

Collaboration: how you worked with others to get it done

Production experience: Many of our teams build and operate large-scale cloud infrastructure, so we’re interested in your experience running software in production at scale.

How should I prepare for my initial call with the recruiter?

No specific prep needed. Have your resume and the job posting handy, and be ready to talk through your career history, skills, and interest in the role.

How are interviews conducted?

We use Zoom for all our video interviews. Please install Zoom prior to the call. A stable connection, headphones, and a quiet background go a long way. If you face any issues connecting, reach out to your recruiter.

Why do you test algorithm knowledge?

While cracking algorithmic tasks doesn’t always equate to being a successful software engineer, we’ve found that they can be a useful indicator of structured problem-solving, technical fluency and the ability to work through complex engineering challenges.

Knowledge of algorithms can come in handy, especially when designing high-performance systems. A system might not start as high-load, but it’s important to establish some early groundwork that will allow it to handle a significant load when needed. A strong understanding of algorithms can play an important role in making those design decisions.

Can I use AI or external assistance during interviews?

To ensure a fair and consistent evaluation process, candidates are expected to complete all interviews and assessments using their own knowledge and independent problem-solving. The use of AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.), answer-generating services, or other external assistance to generate or guide responses is not permitted unless explicitly stated otherwise.

This expectation applies to all stages of the interview process, including phone screens, virtual or in-person interviews, and technical or take-home assessments. Candidates may be asked to explain their thinking, walk through their approach, or reproduce their work as part of the evaluation.

Failure to adhere to these guidelines may result in removal from the interview process.

Which technologies and languages should I use during interviews?

Your choice. The main goal here is to see how you think and come up with solutions to different problems.

What happens after each interview?

Your recruiter will follow up within a week to discuss the interview, answer any questions and walk you through next steps.

Can I work remotely?

Most roles are hybrid: in-office with flexibility for 1–2 days at home per week. A small number of teams have more flexibility than that, but that’s not a common practice overall. Please let your recruiter know during the first call if this matters to you.
