## Research areas

## Inference Optimization

Making large-model serving faster, cheaper, and more scalable on modern GPU clusters.

Proposals welcome on:

- speculative decoding

- KV-cache compression and management

- low-precision inference (FP8/FP4 quantization)

- sparse and long-context attention

- disaggregated prefill/decode architectures

- MoE serving

- kernel- or scheduler-level optimizations

Strong projects pair algorithmic ideas with real systems benchmarks: latency, throughput, and cost per token at scale.

## Post-Training

Turning pretrained models into capable, aligned, and reliable systems.

Directions include:

- RL from human or AI feedback

- RL with verifiable rewards

- reward modeling

- preference optimization (DPO and beyond)

- reasoning and chain-of-thought training

- synthetic data generation and distillation

- rigorous evaluation of post-trained models

Work that improves sample efficiency or makes post-training pipelines more reproducible and open is especially valued.

## Agentic Systems

LLM-based agents that plan, use tools, and act over long horizons.

Topics include:

- multi-agent orchestration

- tool-use and code-execution agents

- memory and long-horizon planning

- RL environments and training for agents

- agent benchmarks and evaluation

- reliability and safety of autonomous systems

Proposals should target measurable improvements on realistic tasks (e.g. software engineering, research automation), not demos alone.

## Eligibility

Applicants should:

- Full-time PhD. Be enrolled full-time in a PhD program at an accredited university

- Strong record. Have a strong research, publication and conference record

- Relevant research. Conduct research relevant to Nebius research areas

- Stay enrolled. Remain enrolled in your PhD program throughout the fellowship

- Internship availability. Be available for the mandatory 3-month internship, before or after the fellowship year (remote or on-site in the Bay Area).

## Evaluation criteria

An internal research committee reviews:

- Research excellence. Publication and conference record

- Alignment. Fit with Nebius research priorities

- Proposal quality. Quality of the proposed research

- Portfolio. Portfolio of projects

- Recommendations. Letters from people who know your work

## FAQ

I’m starting my PhD this year. Can I apply?

We accept candidates starting their 2nd year of the PhD program.

Is the internship mandatory?

Yes. It is a 3-month internship, either remote or on-site in the Bay Area or Amsterdam, where our research team sits. It can take place before or after the fellowship academic year.

Can I work part-time or combine this with another fellowship?

Part-time work is considered case by case and depends on your profile; for some candidates we may allow it. The fellowship cannot be combined with another fellowship at the same time.

Can current Fellows reapply for later years?

No. It is a one-time fellowship.

When will I hear back?

We start reviewing all applications right after the deadline on January 15, 2027. Later, selected candidates will be invited to a final interview with the team. Final decisions are made and Fellows announced at the end of February 2027.

What is considered a strong record?

A strong record means peer-reviewed work at top-tier venues. Papers at conferences such as ICML, ICLR, and NeurIPS are typical examples. We also look at your Google Scholar profile, including your h-index and citation counts, as additional indicators of the impact of your work.

Meeting one of these criteria is not a strict requirement. We review each application as a whole, so please include links to your publications and your Google Scholar profile.
