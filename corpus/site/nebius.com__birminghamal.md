## Project overview

Nebius is developing the former Regions Bank back-office site in Birmingham’s Oxmoor neighborhood for its potential AI factory.​ We are committed to transparency throughout the process, and to building a project that delivers lasting benefits to local communities.

## No impact on home electric bills

Nebius will pay for all electricity and any infrastructure upgrades. This project can help strengthen the local power grid by increasing industrial consumers in APCO’s region.

## Low annual water consumption

Thanks to an innovative closed-loop cooling system design, the facility have minimal ongoing water needs.

## Built to fit in

We’re designing this facility to be a good neighbor. That means noise-reduction technology to keep noise low, and planning construction work to minimize disruption to residents.

## Job creation and support for the local economy

The project will create 1,000+ skilled construction jobs, support local contractors, and offer long-term career opportunities.

## Long-term community investment

The project will generate roughly $85.7M in annual tax revenue for Birmingham City, Jefferson County, and the state of Alabama supporting education, public infrastructure, parks, and essential services. Nebius' community benefits plan would further advance local workforce opportunities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Nebius and why Birmingham?

Nebius is a Nasdaq-listed technology company building next-generation AI cloud. By building and operating large-scale AI factories across the US and Europe, we provide essential infrastructure that delivers the most advanced compute.

Birmingham’s position as a hub for healthcare, advanced manufacturing, and tech innovation aligns with our business strengths, and we are committed to building a long-term, mutually beneficial partnership. We chose to invest in the former Regions Bank back‑office building in Birmingham’s Oxmoor neighborhood for its existing infrastructure and its location within an established business park. By working closely with regional land specialists and technical design experts, we’re confident that this project will transform the vacant property into a valuable and productive asset for the area.

How large is the potential investment from Nebius?

The initial construction phase represents a multi-billion-dollar investment and will create hundreds of skilled construction jobs. Nebius’ projections show that will pay approximately $48 million annually in education taxes for the benefit of Birmingham City and Jefferson County Schools. We remain committed to collaborating with local leaders to ensure our investments are impactful and support initiatives most important to the region.

How will the project be powered? Will it impact local electric rates for consumers?

The project would connect to a new, Nebius-funded switching and substation derived from Alabama Power (APCO) to generate power for our facility. Given this new generation coming online, we won’t impact residential rates. APCO has confirmed energy needs can be met without power reliability issues or rate hikes.

How much water will this project use?

We are planning to use a closed-loop or other similarly low-consumption cooling systems, consistent with the approach used at our other facilities. While final numbers will depend on detailed engineering, daily water usage is estimated to be about a quarter of what the former Regions building consumed.

Are you requesting a tax abatement, and what approvals will you need?

Nebius has received tax incentives for 30 years through the Industrial Development Board. The project will generate on average $85.7M in annual tax revenue for Birmingham City, Jefferson County, and the state of Alabama, making it one of the most significant economic contributors in the region.

What would construction look like?

Construction is expected to take place in phases over an approximately 30-month time frame. We are working with Birmingham-based Hoar Construction as a general contractor and anticipate creating hundreds of highly skilled construction jobs during the buildout.

What commitments is Nebius prepared to make to the community?

Nebius has solicited feedback through a community survey and is currently working with local leaders to establish a community benefits plan framework that centers on advancing education. We want to partner with the community on areas to invest, volunteer, and deliver impactful actions.

Additionally, we have established a Community Engagement Panel of representative community voices to foster dialogue between residents and community as the project evolves.

What kind of customers and workloads will be serviced in this facility?

Nebius is an AI-cloud company that provides full-stack support exclusively for AI workloads and we design our infrastructure and software solutions to meet those specific needs.

Work done at this facility will service our customers’ needs — from advanced medical research to enterprise business solutions. Some of the work done to date on our Nebius infrastructure and cloud platform has led to a breakthrough in Alzheimer’s diagnosis and genomic research, for example. Read more about the medical breakthroughs AI is enabling.

Will the AI factory produce excess noise?

No. An independent sound study conducted by Affiliated Engineers, Inc. (AEI) found that the facility will not generate noise beyond the area’s existing background levels.

As part of the study, AEI measured sound volume and frequencies at multiple locations and compared them to recorded baseline noise levels. Their analysis confirmed that our sound mitigation measures will work as designed and any noise from the facility will remain within today’s ambient noise levels and not increase the overall level in the community.

What safety protocols will be put in place at the facility?

The facility will be designed and operated in full compliance with all applicable emergency preparedness, environmental protection, and waste management regulations to safeguard both the community and the environment. Our team has been working closely with municipal officials and engineering experts to ensure comprehensive planning and strong coordination for emergency response procedures.

How will hardware components be handled once they’ve reached end-of-life?

Running a large-scale compute infrastructure means we do need to retire hardware that’s reached the end of its useful life. Wherever possible, we send components for recycling to keep materials in circulation. This creates a longer lasting impact by easing the demand for new raw materials across the supply chain and reducing the strain on natural resources. Read more about Nebius’ approach to sustainability in our latest report.
